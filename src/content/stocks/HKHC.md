In 1951, Ida Goldstein bought the formula for Scott's Liquid Gold, a furniture polish, from a door-to-door salesman for $350. Her sons started mixing and bottling it in a small garage in East Denver and selling it door to door. Scott's Liquid Gold grew into a public company.

<h2>Then it became an investment firm</h2>
On August 1, 2024, Scott's Liquid Gold merged with Horizon Kinetics, a New York investment firm. The owners of Horizon Kinetics received 96.5% of the shares, leaving the old Scott's shareholders with about 3.5%. The company changed its name to Horizon Kinetics Holding Corporation. So a furniture polish company is now an asset manager.

One note: the stock doesn't trade on a stock exchange. It is quoted as HKHC on the OTCQX tier of the OTC Markets.

This is a fun profile, not investment advice.
