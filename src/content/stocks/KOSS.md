Before earbuds, before the Walkman, there was a pair of headphones from Milwaukee. Koss Corporation says its founder, John C. Koss, created the personal listening industry when the company made its first SP/3 stereo headphones in 1958. The company is still around, still based in Milwaukee, and its ticker is simply KOSS.

<h2>Still small, still headphones</h2>
Koss says its only business is designing, making, and selling stereo headphones and related gear. It buys finished headphones from manufacturers in Asia and also brings in raw materials to make headphones at its plant in Milwaukee. Some of its products go to school systems, prisons, and other companies.

It's a small operation. Sales for the year that ended June 30, 2026 were about $13.0 million, up 3.1% from the year before.

<h2>A big pile of patents</h2>
For a company that size, Koss holds a lot of paperwork. As of June 30, 2026, it had about 400 trademarks registered in roughly 88 countries and nearly 170 patents in about 25 countries. It has sued a number of companies it believes infringed its patents, including patents tied to wireless audio, and it has granted licenses to some of them. In fiscal 2026, the licensing money offset the legal bills.

<h2>Now it wants to shop</h2>
Here's the twist. In 2026, Koss announced a "diversification by acquisition" plan. It hired a Director of Acquisitions and said it wants to buy strong, cash-generating companies outside of consumer electronics and hold them "for the long-term, and preferably forever." The company says there's no guarantee any deals will happen.

<h2>A family company with wild trading days</h2>
The Koss family still runs the show. CEO and Chairman Michael Koss beneficially owned 40.6% of the shares as of August 1, 2026. The stock can also get jumpy. In fiscal 2026, daily trading ranged from just 5,600 shares on April 10, 2026 to more than 6.5 million shares on July 23, 2025, and the company's annual report even warns about the risk of a "short squeeze."
