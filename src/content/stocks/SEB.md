Seaboard Corporation started small. It was founded in 1918 as a flour brokerage business, and its own timeline begins with its first flour mill in Atchison, Kansas. Over the next hundred years it kept adding businesses that don't seem to have much to do with each other.

<h2>A very mixed bag</h2>
Today Seaboard raises hogs and processes pork in the U.S., makes biofuel, trades commodities and processes grain in Africa and South America, and runs cargo shipping in the U.S., the Caribbean and Central and South America. It also owns a stake in Butterball, the turkey company. And it makes electricity in the Dominican Republic from power plants that float on barges, including a 71.2 megawatt barge-mounted plant.

<h2>Not many shares</h2>
Seaboard is also unusual on the stock market. It had just 957,794 shares outstanding as of January 31, 2026, and its annual report used a closing price of $2,843.29 a share from June 2025. That's a lot for a single share.
