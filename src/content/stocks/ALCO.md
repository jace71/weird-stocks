For a long time, Alico was known for oranges. Then it stopped growing them.

<h2>Goodbye, citrus</h2>
On January 6, 2025, Alico announced a "Strategic Transformation." After many seasons of trouble from citrus greening disease and other environmental problems, it decided to stop spending serious money on its citrus groves and wound down most of its citrus operations after the 2024-2025 harvest ended in April 2025.

<h2>Hello, real estate</h2>
Now Alico calls itself an agricultural land leasing and land development company. As of September 30, 2026, it owned about 47,224 acres in seven Florida counties, plus about 42,400 acres of oil, gas and mineral rights. Its most advanced project is Corkscrew Grove Villages, planned on about 4,600 acres in eastern Collier County. On April 28, 2026, the county commissioners voted unanimously to approve the first piece, the 1,446-acre Corkscrew Grove East Village.
