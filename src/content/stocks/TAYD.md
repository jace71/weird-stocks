When the ground shakes, buildings need something to soak up the energy. Taylor Devices makes that something. Its seismic dampers are designed to reduce the effects of earthquakes on structures, and they make up a big part of the company's sales.

<h2>Lots of ways to absorb a shock</h2>
Taylor Devices was incorporated in New York on July 22, 1955. It sells nine families of products, including seismic dampers, tiny shock absorbers called Fluidicshoks, crane buffers, liquid die springs, vibration dampers and custom actuators. Custom versions of these go into aerospace and defense projects.

It's a niche business, but a useful one: the company's whole job is making sudden jolts a little gentler.
