Most police gear is about stopping someone. Wrap Technologies sells a gadget that ties them up instead. Its main product, the BolaWrap 150, is a handheld device that shoots out a Kevlar tether to wrap around a person and hold them in place from a distance. And yes, the ticker is WRAP.

<h2>What it actually does</h2>
Wrap calls the BolaWrap 150 a "remote restraint device." The idea is to give officers a way to get control of someone who won't cooperate while keeping some space between them, early in an encounter, before things get worse. The device uses refill cassettes, so the company also sells those, plus training.

The company says the BolaWrap is used by more than 1,000 agencies in the U.S. and in more than 60 countries.

<h2>Officially not a weapon</h2>
Here's the strange part. In July 2026, the U.S. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) ruled that the BolaWrap 150 is an "instrument of restraint." Under federal law, it is not a firearm and not an "any other weapon." So it's a device that launches a tether at people, and the government says it isn't a weapon.

<h2>What it's working on now</h2>
Wrap also sells a virtual reality training simulator called Wrap Reality, an online training platform called WrapTactics, and a body camera system called WrapVision. In November 2026, Illinois certified its BolaWrap user course for officer training, the first state to do so.

Next up is WrapShield, a platform meant to tie together threat detection, AI-assisted decision support, and a response. Its first target is counter-drone work. The company says these plans are early and it can't say when they'll bring in money.

It's still a small company. Revenue for the third quarter of 2026 was $1.5 million, about the same as a year earlier.
