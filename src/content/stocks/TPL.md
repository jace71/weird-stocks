In 1871, the Texas and Pacific Railway got a federal charter to build a railroad from Texas to California, with land granted for every mile of track it built. It finished 972 miles by 1881, which entitled it to 3.5 million acres of Texas land. Then the railroad went bankrupt. In 1888, that land was put into a trust for the railroad's bondholders, and the trust's certificates were listed on the NYSE.

<h2>Still sitting on the land</h2>
That trust is now Texas Pacific Land Corporation. It reorganized from a trust into a corporation in 2021, and it still holds about 896,000 surface acres, mostly in the Permian Basin. It isn't an oil producer. Instead, it gets paid along the way: fees for using its land, sales of water and caliche, oil and gas royalties, and easements for pipelines and power lines. It has also invested in Bolt Data & Energy to develop data center campuses on its land.

<h2>A busy November</h2>
On November 17, 2026, the company moved its legal home from Delaware to Texas. Days later, on November 23, 2026, it announced a deal to buy EagleRock Land, which would bring it to about 1 million surface acres.
