Most companies on the New York Stock Exchange have a CEO, a staff and an office full of people. Mesabi Trust has none of that. Its own annual report says the trust "does not have any directors, officers or employees." A corporate trustee handles the record keeping and the cash.

<h2>What it actually does</h2>
Mesabi Trust collects royalties on iron ore. Cleveland-Cliffs, the parent of Northshore Mining Company, pays the trust based on iron ore shipped out of Silver Bay, Minnesota. The trust passes that money to the people who own its units, which trade on the NYSE as MSB.

<h2>Some quarters, nothing</h2>
Because the money depends on iron ore shipments and prices, the checks swing a lot. On October 15, 2026, the trustees said no distribution would be paid in November 2026, compared with 34 cents a unit for the same period a year earlier. Part of the reason: the trust received no bonus royalties in 2026, because Cliffs reported that the shipments were priced below the bonus threshold of $71.70 a ton. On October 30, 2026, the trust received another $3,071,304 in royalty payments.
